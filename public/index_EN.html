The Corpus Author Ontology CRM (CAO_CRM) Ontological Model

Presentation and user guide for a structured semantic framework for the organisation, description and interoperability of metadata generated for textual corpora

General Presentation

The CAO_CRM ontological model, developed by the Consortium HN Ariane, aims to model the relationships between metadata associated with cultural or intellectual objects, such as documents, literary works or digital resources.

Building on the CIDOC CRM, an international standard for modelling cultural heritage knowledge, this model also integrates the LRM (Library Reference Model) and CRMdig (CRM Digital) extensions, which respectively enable the representation of bibliographic entities and the provenance of digital objects.

Designed to operate in synergy with artificial intelligence systems — particularly in the context of RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation), a method combining documentary retrieval with AI-generated content — as used in the AMIS web application, this ontological model provides a structured semantic framework for organising, describing and ensuring the interoperability of textual corpus metadata. It addresses key issues such as the standardisation of descriptive practices, the identification of relevant metadata according to resource types, and the optimisation of documentary retrieval through the exploitation of semantic relationships.

A Structured and Simple Organisation

The model is organised around a structural backbone — a column of entities ensuring the model's internal consistency. This organisation facilitates its adaptation to different types of content and usage contexts. Thanks to its flexibility and capacity for evolution, it can serve many purposes: from the detailed description of heritage works to the feeding of recommendation systems or automated analysis tools in fields such as research, education or cultural valorisation.

It makes it possible to organise and analyse precise metadata while taking into account missing or uncertain data, which is essential when dealing with cultural or intellectual objects that are often only partially documented. This adaptability is a major asset for artificial intelligence systems and automated analysis tools, which require a fine-grained and dynamic understanding of objects and the relationships that characterise them.

Document Objectives

This document aims to explain in detail the various branches of the ontological model and its interrelations, illustrating these concepts through a concrete example: Stendhal's literary work The Red and the Black. This approach clarifies the methodological choices underlying the modelling, specifying the entities and relationships used to represent the different facets of the work and its associated objects. The example also serves to test the relevance and robustness of the model against a complex case. It should be noted that some of the information used to populate the ontology does not always come from explicitly documented or verifiable data. Consequently, such information should be regarded not as absolute truth, but as plausible hypotheses illustrating the model's flexibility. The process described highlights the ontological model's capacity to support the integration of different levels of knowledge — explicit or implicit — while allowing a degree of interpretive latitude in the handling of metadata. This approach is essential for confronting the model with real-world situations, where gaps or uncertainties may exist in the available data, and for testing its robustness in managing such ambiguities.

1. Structure and Modularity of CAO_CRM

Modular Architecture

CAO_CRM rests on a stable, coherent, and above all generalised architecture. This generality provides an overall view of the structure, which can be used in its entirety or with certain model entities omitted depending on the available data and the desired level of granularity. Such an approach is particularly useful when information about a work is incomplete or fragmentary. Thanks to its sophisticated relational mechanisms, the model maintains an overall CAO coherence.

The architecture establishes semantic links between different entities. These connections make it possible to infer implicit information from explicit data. For instance, from the identification of a physical object — such as a manuscript — the model can infer information about the underlying intellectual work (F1_Work) or its author (E21_Person), either from the rest of the information present in the model or by querying external databases (Wikidata, Geonames, etc.). These inferences are made possible through ontological properties and relationships inherited from international standards such as CIDOC CRM, LRM, and CRMdig, which together ensure both the logical robustness and the semantic coherence of the model.

A Simplified and Didactic View

The CAO_CRM model is presented in this document in a simplified form, designed specifically for exploratory and pedagogical purposes. This simplified view aims to make the fundamental semantic relationships and key properties that may exist between the various conceptual entities explicit. It thus offers a structured framework for reflecting on the model's general architecture, with emphasis placed on its essential components and on the elements that may be included or omitted depending on the specific needs of each project.

In the interest of readability and accessibility, the document does not present the full set of entities and relationships, which are often numerous and complex. The following description is intended as a clear and comprehensible conceptual basis, enabling an understanding of the model's potential. The complete version of the model can indeed become dense and difficult to grasp without the aid of dedicated computational tools.

Methodology and Fundamental Principles

The model rests on a clear and fundamental distinction between key entities, in accordance with the LRMoo, CRMdig, and CIDOC CRM standards:

F1_Work – Intellectual Work

This is the abstract idea or concept of a work, independent of any material form or specific realisation. This entity represents the creative intention or the work in its purely conceptual dimension.

F2_Expression

This entity refers to the intellectual shaping of a work: the reflection, imagination, and drafts (the various conceptual "states" of elaboration). It may have one or more manifestations.

F3_Manifestation

This is the tangible materialisation of an expression, such as an autograph manuscript, a printed book, an edited film or an audiovisual edition. It presupposes a process that makes the expression accessible to the public.

F5_Item

This entity refers to the material carrier containing the manifestation, such as a folio, a book or a film reel. It is the object that can be owned and handled.

D1_Digital_Object

Unlike F5_Item, this entity refers to the digital carrier containing the manifestation (digital file, XML-TEI). It may be a digitisation of a physical object or a born-digital document.

D2_Digitization_Process

This entity encompasses the entire process of digitising a physical work to produce an identical digital copy, enabling in particular its encoding for linguistic or metrical analyses.

Entity Hierarchy

The entity hierarchy follows a logical progression from the original idea of a work through to its tangible or digital materialisation:

  • F1_Work → F2_Expression: The intellectual work (F1_Work) is the originating concept — an idea or creative project. It gives rise to an Expression (F2_Expression), its first immaterial concretisation in a specific form.
  • F2_Expression → F3_Manifestation: The Expression can be materialised in tangible or digital form, becoming a Manifestation. This step corresponds to the physical or digital version of the Expression, such as a printed book, a sound recording or an edited film.
  • F3_Manifestation → E12_Production / D2_Digitization_Process: For a physical or digital object to be created, a creative action is necessary. This action records all the elements that led to the production of a result (F5_Item / D1_Digital_Object) taking the Manifestation as its source.
  • E12_Production / D2_Digitization_Process → F5_Item / D1_Digital_Object: The production process results in a physical or digital carrier that stores all the elements expressed in the Manifestation. This carrier contains the work in a tangible or digital form, enabling the user to consult, own or use it.
  • D2_Digitization_Process → F5_Item / D1_Digital_Object: The process of digitising a physical work to create a digital copy is the element that links the two entities. This process, often omitted from current metadata, is here made explicit, allowing temporal boundaries and a digitisation actor to be defined.

This overview highlights the logical and hierarchical progression of entities, moving from the abstract idea of a work to its materialisation as a physical or digital carrier. This structure ensures a coherent evolution from concept to object, and a semantic and structural continuity between the different stages of the model.

The hierarchical and modular architecture of CAO_CRM makes it possible to model a wide diversity of cultural and intellectual objects with great precision and flexibility. It preserves a strong coherence between the different levels of description, thereby facilitating data management while remaining adaptable to varied needs. Thanks to this hierarchy, it also becomes possible to adjust the level of detail according to the specific requirements of each project, domain of application or available data, while guaranteeing a homogeneous and interoperable approach to metadata.

2. The Entities of CAO_CRM

The Different Categories of Metadata

In the interest of clarity and to remain as close as possible to editorial practices — particularly when editing a TEI header — we have created different categories of metadata:

  • Characteristic: This category includes all metadata used to describe a physical or digital object: an identifier (title, DOI), dimensions, the material used, a description of the object, the writing system, the format, the language, and the type of arrangement of the work. In a TEI Header, these elements correspond to the FileDesc and SourceDesc.
  • Process: All metadata identifying and qualifying a creative process are listed here: the author of the qualified creation, the date, the place, the type of process used, and a description.
  • Status: The legal aspects of a work and the various stages of its creation are also described (intellectual property, rights-holders' rights, editorial rights, etc.). The place of storage and the conservation status are also described elements.
  • Relation: Finally, all links between a work and another, or whether the work under study references other works, can be described — thereby highlighting the connections between different works.

All of these metadata are present around the entities, at different levels of description, and serve to clarify and complement TEI Headers or DCterms, for example, thus making explicit the rationale behind the creation of this ontological model.

The "Artistic Work" (F1_Work)

This section of the ontological model is dedicated to the representation of the work as an abstract entity, along with all the conceptual elements that surround it. The F1_Work entity, derived from CIDOC CRM, identifies a work as the result of an intellectual process, independent of any physical manifestation. It therefore does not refer to the material object, a version, or the process of writing the work, but rather to the idea and reflections that led to its elaboration — even before the first draft.

The F1_Work class belongs to the domain of the abstract and enables different forms of expression of the same work to be linked. A title (E35_Title) identifies the work and the totality of the artist's creative output by a commonly used name. In addition to its identification, a description of the rights (E30_Right) and the type (E55_Type) of right specifically applied to the work is made explicit, in order to clarify the applicable rights depending on the object being qualified.

The F27_Work_Creation entity is associated with F1_Work and documents the process of creating the work. It accounts for the activities that led to the elaboration of the work as an intellectual object, including the period of creation (E52_Time-Span) as well as the person or persons who participated in this process (E21_Person).

In the case of The Red and the Black, these data include the date of the work's creation, the historical period to which it belongs, and the literary movement (E89_Propositional_Object) in which it is situated. The use of common reference systems such as Periodo or Wikidata ensures interpretive consistency and guarantees the interoperability of the ontological model. The modelling of the work's creation also makes it possible to link the work to its authors (E21_Person), drawing on authority databases such as IdRef, Wikidata or VIAF.

  • (CIDOC CRM) E89_Propositional_Object: Literary movement "Romanticism".

The "Artistic Creation" (F2_Expression)

The expression (F2_Expression) is an intermediate entity that encompasses the intellectual or artistic realisations of works in the form of identifiable immaterial objects, such as texts, poems, multimedia objects and any other artistic form. The same work (F1_Work) may have different expressions (F2_Expression). These are not dependent on a specific physical carrier and may exist simultaneously on one or more supports — only their form is characteristic and proper to each expression, allowing them to be distinguished from one another. Differences of language or mode of execution also imply different expressions (for example, translations or arrangements for different instruments).

In the case of The Red and the Black, the final, original version produced by Stendhal will differ from all the critical editions made a posteriori. The expression (F2_Expression) is a conceptual entity that brings together a condensation of the artist's ideas in a desired form. As such, the rights remain with the artist and fall under intellectual property and copyright law.

The "Manifestation" (F3_Manifestation)

In the CAO_CRM ontological model, a manifestation (represented by the class F3_Manifestation) corresponds to the stage at which one or more expressions (F2_Expression) are brought together into products. This notion of manifestation is inspired by the LRMoo model and refers to the giving of form to an expression of the work.

The F3_Manifestation structures and aggregates information relating to this future materialisation, providing crucial details about the materiality and overall content of the forthcoming physical work. In the model, a manifestation is directly linked to a manifestation creation event (F30_Manifestation_Creation), which describes the specific circumstances of the copy's production.

Key properties of the F3_Manifestation branch:

  • Relationships: A manifestation may reference another manifestation within its content, or have an alternative manifestation. References to other manifestations or works by other artists may be present within the described manifestation.
  • Rights: The rights applied to a manifestation differ from those applied to the expression (F2_Expression) or the artistic work (F1_Work). These are often the rights of the publisher or printer.
  • Process: For the manifestation entity to exist, an activity enabling its creation is necessary, which identifies the chronological boundaries, the place of creation, and the natural or legal persons responsible.
  • Characteristics: The manifestation has characteristics that distinguish it: the language used in the work, its identifier, and the type of manifestation.

  • (LRMoo) F3_Manifestation: A manifestation of The Red and the Black.

  • (CIDOC CRM) E55_Type (manifestation type): Typescript (printed book, in-18).
  • (LRMoo) F30_Manifestation_Creation: Printing process of the original edition.

3. The Two Pathways of CAO_CRM

The "Physical Object" (F5_Item)

Within the CAO_CRM ontological model, the item (F5_Item) represents a specific physical copy of a work, making it possible to document its existence, conservation and transformations. This manuscript, along with the author's personal archives (including drafts, revised versions and corrections), can be modelled across different levels: from the work as an abstract entity (F1_Work) to its first expressions (F2_Expression), through its materialisation in the form of a manifestation (F3_Manifestation), and finally its specific physical copy (F5_Item).

For the physical object (F5_Item) to exist, an event is necessary. The production (E12_Production) of the physical object was thus required. This step describes various elements, including the natural or legal person (E39_Actor) responsible for the edition, the mode of production (E55_Type), the place (E53_Place) and date (E52_Time-Span) of production. This activity marks the boundary between the immaterial and the material.

The physical object is described by concrete metadata complementing archival descriptions: its identifier (E42_Identifier) and type (E55_Type), its dimensions (E54_Dimension), material (E57_Material), arrangement type (E55_Type) according to the thesaurus of textual genres, as well as a description (E62_String) and the writing system used. To these characteristics are added the status: rights (E30_Right), physical condition (E3_Condition_State), conservation status (E55_Type) and place of storage (E53_Place).

Finally, a special process is described: the scientific activity (E7_Activity), regarded as a production event in its own right, where a scientific responsible (E21_Person), the type of scientific activity (E55_Type) and a description (E62_Description) can be named, adding any information that cannot be described by the entities previously mentioned.

The Material Traces of Creation

The ontological model makes it possible to capture the full complexity of relationships between these different entities, particularly with regard to the material traces of the work's creation. These links are captured through specific entities such as:

  • F2_Expression: This entity links a manuscript (a specific version of the work) to the original work. This expression is an integral part of the creative process that gave rise to the published work.
  • F3_Manifestation: When a manuscript is formalised in printed form, it takes the form of a manifestation. For example, a first printed edition of The Red and the Black can be catalogued as a distinct manifestation, while remaining linked to the underlying text expression.

The "Digitization Process" (CRMdig)

In the CAO_CRM model, it is possible to describe two specific types of elements: a physical object and a digital object. The latter encompasses all born-digital documents — that is, documents created digitally with no physical equivalent — as well as digitisations of physical objects.

This digitisation process is an intermediate phase between a physical object and a digital object, as it enables the creation of a faithful copy of a manuscript in digital format. In particular, within the context of a scientific activity focused on textual study, it allows annotations, statistical analyses, and automated language processing to be carried out without altering the original form.

Digitisation and Digital Preservation

Manuscript drafts or other physical objects may be digitised to ensure their preservation and digital accessibility. This process can be modelled as follows:

  • The physical object (F5_Item) is described by metadata qualifying it as a physical object in its own right. A scientific activity (E7_Activity) is also described, highlighting the scholarly work carried out on the physical object alone.
  • The digitisation process (D2_Digitization_Process) of a physical object is documented by information such as the person in charge of the digitisation (E21_Person), the digitisation period (E52_Time-Span), and the process type (E55_Type), which provides details regarding the digitisation methods and tools used.
  • The digitisation process (D2_Digitization_Process) enables the creation of a digital object (D1_Digital_Object), which in turn has its own metadata.

Thus, the digitisation process creates a digital twin of a physical object. This digital double also has an archival dimension, as it protects the physical object from further handling while enabling simplified dissemination of documents accessible from any computer in the world.

Digitisation

  • (CIDOC CRM) E55_Type (process type): OCR and IIIF.
  • (CIDOC CRM) E21_Person: BNF staff.

The "Digital Object" (D1_Digital_Object)

The contemporary era is marked by the progressive digitisation of content, which introduces a new category of cultural objects. Today, literary works exist not only as physical objects but also as born-digital or digitised objects. Although these digital objects differ in their material nature, they retain a documentary and intellectual value equivalent to that of physical objects.

The integration of these digital objects into the CAO_CRM ontological model is essential for ensuring the coherence and interoperability of metadata. This branch is structured around the entity F3_Manifestation, which makes it possible to associate a digital object with a precise representation of the intellectual work. The creation of the digital edition is represented by a production phase (D2_Digitization_Process) describing the persons, technical conditions and temporality under which the digital expression of the work was realised.

The D1_Digital_Object entity can be enriched by a series of technical and contextual properties, such as:

  • The file format (e.g. PDF, EPUB, TXT).
  • The title or identifier assigned to the digital object.
  • The file description.
  • The writing system used in the file (Roman, Cyrillic alphabet, etc.).
  • Digital dimensions, in particular the storage volume required.
  • The place of storage or dissemination platform (e.g. Gallica, Internet Archive, institutional repository).
  • Rights pertaining to the online publication of these files.
  • Any possible scientific activity.

Summary of Relationships

  • F1_Work → F2_Expression: The intellectual work (F1_Work) is expressed through one or more expressions (F2_Expression) such as manuscript drafts or other intermediate creations.
  • F2_Expression → F3_Manifestation: These expressions may be published in the form of physical or digital manifestations (F3_Manifestation).
  • F3_Manifestation → F5_Item: Each manifestation takes a tangible form, or a specific copy, which becomes an F5_Item (for example, a physical manuscript).
  • D2_Digitization_Process → D1_Digital_Object: A physical Item can be digitised, giving rise to digital objects (D1_Digital_Object) that reproduce the pages of the manuscript or edition.
  • OR F3_Manifestation → D1_Digital_Object: Each manifestation may also directly take a digital form, which becomes a D1_Digital_Object (for example, a digital edition).

Logical Progression of the Model

Each stage of the process is therefore detailed in the model:

F1_Work → F2_Expression → F3_Manifestation
  • F1_Work (Intellectual Work): This entity represents the abstract idea and fundamental concept of the work, independent of any physical materialisation.
  • F2_Expression (Expression): An Expression is an immaterial form of the work, arising from intellectual creativity. In our example, Stendhal's manuscript drafts are expressions of the work that embody the first phase of its concretisation before it became a published text.
  • F3_Manifestation (Manifestation): Manifestations are tangible copies of the work in a specific form. For The Red and the Black, the first printed edition of 1830 constitutes a physical manifestation, accompanied by a creation event (F30_Manifestation_Creation) documenting the place of publication, the publisher and the date.
  • F5_Item (Physical Object): An Item represents a specific copy of a manifestation. This includes classic object management metadata such as the publication date, material, physical dimensions and title.
  • D1_Digital_Object (Digital Object): A Digital Object represents a specific copy of a manifestation in its digital form. This includes classic digital object management metadata such as the upload date, output format, digital dimensions and title.

This detailed approach to the ontological model makes it possible to capture the entirety of the creation process — from the first manuscript draft to the final printed version — while ensuring effective preservation through the digitisation of physical objects and vice versa.
